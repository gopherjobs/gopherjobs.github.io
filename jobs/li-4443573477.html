About The Role Callosum believes that orders of magnitude improvements in AI systems will come through application-aware orchestration across heterogeneous hardware. Today, computational workloads are largely mapped onto predefined execution environments, with limited ability to exploit the diversity of emerging accelerators. We are building infrastructure that treats compute as a unified, dynamic system, where workloads can be understood and executed according to the capabilities of the hardware available.

This role sits at the centre of Callosum's orchestration stack, determining how computation is distributed and coordinated across heterogeneous resources. You will extend the algorithms and systems that translate computational workloads into efficient execution strategies, working across scheduling, resource allocation, performance modelling, and representations of computation. Drawing on ideas from distributed systems, optimisation, and compilers, you will help define how heterogeneous compute operates as one system rather than a collection of independent machines.

What You'll Build

  • Develop orchestration systems that coordinate computational workloads across heterogeneous accelerators and distributed infrastructure.
  • Design scheduling and resource allocation algorithms that optimise performance, cost, and utilisation across diverse hardware.
  • Extend mechanisms for representing and transforming workloads into efficient distributed execution strategies.
  • Develop workload-aware optimisation techniques that respond to changing demand and system conditions.
  • Work across inference engines, networking, and accelerator systems to translate orchestration decisions into efficient execution.

What Sets You Apart

  • Strong experience building distributed systems, scheduling infrastructure, or computational execution frameworks.
  • Deep understanding of resource allocation, scheduling algorithms, and performance trade-offs in large-scale systems.
  • Familiarity with compiler concepts, computational graphs, query planning, or other approaches to representing and transforming computation.
  • Experience reasoning about dynamic workloads, system performance, and optimisation under resource constraints.
  • Strong systems engineering instincts, with an ability to design abstractions that generalise across unfamiliar hardware and workloads.
